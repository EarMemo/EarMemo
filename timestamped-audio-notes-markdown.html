EarMemo › Timestamped notes while listening

Take notes at the exact timestamp while listening — and get them out as Markdown

EarMemo lets you write a note without leaving playback, stamped with the exact second you were at. Tap the marker later and the audio jumps back to that moment. Notes export as plain Markdown — one note, one episode, or a whole album merged — so they drop straight into Obsidian. Nothing is uploaded or transcribed.

Last updated 2026-09-10 · Written by Fan YANG, the developer of EarMemo.

The problem this solves

You hear one good sentence at 3:12:40 of a nine-hour book. Six months later you remember the idea and not the book. The usual workaround is a bookmark plus a scavenger hunt: find the file, scrub around, find the sentence, retype it somewhere. A timestamp-anchored note collapses that into one action taken while the sentence is still in your ear.

How it works in EarMemo

  1. Drop a marker while listening. The marker records the exact playback position. Markers are unlimited, including in the free version.
  2. Write on it, then or later. Notes auto-save. You can add a marker now and put words on it when you get to a keyboard.
  3. Tap the marker to jump back. Playback resumes at that exact second, so you can re-hear the sentence before you quote it.
  4. Export as Markdown. A single note, one episode's notes, or every note in an album merged into one file. Plain .md, no proprietary container.

How this differs from the AI podcast-note apps

Tools like Snipd and Onda are built around transcription: your audio (or a reference to it) goes to a server, comes back as text, and AI summarises it. That is a genuinely different product with real advantages — searchable transcripts, automatic summaries — and real costs.

Getting notes into Obsidian

There is no integration and there will not be one — EarMemo makes no network requests. The export is deliberately boring: it hands you .md files with the timestamps written in, and you put them in your vault the way you would any other file (AirDrop, Files, iCloud Drive, the share sheet). Because they are plain Markdown, they also work in Bear, Logseq, Notion, or a text editor.

What the free version includes

Markers are unlimited for everyone. The free version lets you write text on five of them; the one-time Full Unlock removes that limit and enables Markdown export. Nothing you wrote is ever deleted or hidden by hitting a limit.

When EarMemo is the wrong choice

  • You want transcripts. EarMemo does not transcribe. There is no speech-to-text, on-device or otherwise.
  • You want an AI to summarise the episode. No AI features at all, by design.
  • You want notes to sync to Obsidian automatically. Export is a manual step producing files. Anything automatic would mean a network connection, which this app does not make.
  • You listen inside a podcast app via RSS. EarMemo plays downloaded files; it is not a podcast client and has no feed subscriptions.

Questions people actually ask

Can I take notes with timestamps while listening on iPhone?

Yes. EarMemo lets you drop a marker at the exact playback position without stopping playback, then write on it immediately or later. Tapping the marker jumps the audio back to that second.

Can I export audio notes to Obsidian?

Yes, as files. EarMemo exports notes as plain Markdown — a single note, one episode, or a whole album merged — which you move into your vault yourself. There is no live sync or integration, because the app never connects to a network.

Does EarMemo transcribe audio?

No. There is no transcription and no AI. The notes are the ones you write yourself; the app's contribution is anchoring them to the exact second and getting them back out as Markdown.

Do the notes survive if I delete the audio file?

Yes. Notes and bookmarks are stored separately from the audio, so clearing files to save space does not take your notes with it.

Does it work for lectures and voice memos too?

Yes. Anything you can import — lecture mp3s, the audio track of an mp4 recording, interviews, voice memos — takes timestamped notes the same way.

Is note-taking free?

Markers are unlimited in the free version, with text on five of them. The one-time Full Unlock removes the writing limit and enables Markdown export.

Try it with your own files
